How to read this demo

Plain language definitions for every panel, slider, and metric.

The story in one paragraph

For a two-layer regularized network f(x) = aT φ(B x), the learned feature matrix H = BTB tracks an AGOP-style gradient geometry. Near the end of training, the most stable relation is the residual-weighted version H2 ≈ κeff G̃, where G̃ is the AGOP weighted by squared residuals. The raw AGOP relation is an intermediate-regime story that breaks down near interpolation when a bridge scalar βfit collapses.

The panels

  • Trajectory plot: real per-checkpoint history from one logged run per family. Loss on the left axis, your chosen overlay on the right. The dashed vertical line marks the best-stationarity checkpoint reported in the paper.
  • Feature alignment heatmap: |B B*T| at the chosen snapshot. Bright cells mean a student hidden unit aligned with a teacher hidden unit.
  • Spectrum overlay: top eigenvalues of H2 versus κeff G̃ at the chosen snapshot. The two should overlap at convergence.
  • Toy sweep plot: a closed-form analytic proxy that recomputes from the sliders. This is the pedagogical part. The active sliders for the current sweep are labeled in green under the title.
  • Failure-map locator: places the current slider state in one of four algebraic regimes (Benign, Beta collapse, Pair failure, Compound).

The metrics, in plain language

  • Weighted residual: ‖H2 - κeff G̃‖ relative to ‖H2‖. The headline success metric.
  • Raw residual: ‖G - ceff H2‖ with ceff = 1 / (κeff βfit). The deterministic raw-AGOP relation. Diverges late in training as βfit collapses.
  • Raw vs weighted overlay: the central two-regime chart. Both residuals on the same axes show that the weighted relation is the stable late-training law while the raw relation degrades.
  • Theorem bound ratio: observed weighted residual divided by the deterministic upper bound. Below 1 means the theorem covers the observation.
  • Beta fit / mean(r2): tests whether the bridge from weighted AGOP to raw AGOP is just residual energy. Stays close to 1 in the reported runs.
  • Apair: a worst-case support-normalized pair-geometry diagnostic. Important theoretically but conservative.
  • Pushed pair error: pair error after applying the learned feature map. This is what actually enters the weighted law.
  • R2: 1 - mean(r2) / var(y). Standard explained-variance metric for model quality. Always shown in the readout cards regardless of the selected overlay.

The four failure regimes

  • Benign: both bridges hold. The weighted law tracks H2 with controllable error. All three reported families land here.
  • Beta collapse: βfit is mis-tracking residual energy because leverage and residuals are correlated. The weighted-to-raw conversion is unreliable.
  • Pair failure: a worst-pair direction also has high stationarity gain, so pushed pair error blows up.
  • Compound: both bridges break at once.

Sweep table regime column

For the regime sweep, each row is a hypothetical checkpoint at a different residual energy. The "regime" column is a label based on raw sensitivity, defined as weighted error divided by βfit.

  • raw OK: raw sensitivity is small, the raw AGOP conversion is stable here. Typical of intermediate training.
  • use weighted: raw sensitivity is large, dividing by βfit blows up. Stick to the weighted law. Typical of late training, near interpolation.
