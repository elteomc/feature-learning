\section{A weighted AGOP law from stationarity}
\label{sec:weighted-law}

We study a two-layer model
\[
f(x)=a^\top \phi(Bx),
\qquad
B\in\mathbb R^{m\times d},
\]
trained with square loss and \(L_2\)-regularization on \(B\):
\[
\mathcal L(B,a)
=
\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^n (f(x_i)-y_i)^2
+
\frac{\lambda}{2}\|B\|_F^2.
\]
The classical neural feature ansatz motivates comparing the learned feature
matrix
\[
H:=B^\top B
\]
to an AGOP-type matrix. Our derivation and experiments suggest that the stable
late-training relation is not primarily a raw AGOP law. It is a
residual-weighted AGOP law.

For each training sample define
\[
r_i := f(x_i)-y_i,
\qquad
q_i := a\odot \phi'(Bx_i).
\]
Let
\[
X := [x_1,\ldots,x_n],
\qquad
Q := [q_1,\ldots,q_n],
\qquad
R := \operatorname{diag}(r_1,\ldots,r_n).
\]
Since \(\nabla f(x_i)=B^\top q_i\), the residual-weighted AGOP is
\[
\widetilde G
:=
B^\top Q R^2 Q^\top B
=
\sum_{i=1}^n r_i^2 \nabla f(x_i)\nabla f(x_i)^\top.
\]

\subsection{Stationarity identity}

The gradient with respect to \(B\) is
\[
\nabla_B\mathcal L
=
\frac1n QRX^\top + \lambda B.
\]
Define the stationarity defect
\[
Z := \lambda n B + QRX^\top.
\]
Then
\[
\lambda n B = -QRX^\top + Z.
\]
With
\[
S:=QRX^\top XRQ^\top,
\qquad
T:=QR^2Q^\top,
\]
we obtain
\[
BB^\top
=
\frac{1}{\lambda^2 n^2}S
+
E_{\mathrm{stat}},
\]
where
\[
E_{\mathrm{stat}}
:=
\frac{1}{\lambda^2 n^2}
\left(
-QRX^\top Z^\top
-
ZXRQ^\top
+
ZZ^\top
\right).
\]
At exact stationarity, \(Z=0\), and therefore
\[
BB^\top
=
\frac{1}{\lambda^2 n^2}S.
\]

\subsection{Effective dimension and pair diagnostics}

The naive isotropic closure would replace \(S\) by \(dT\). This is too crude
under anisotropic or low-rank input families. We instead use the best scalar
fit
\[
d_{\mathrm{eff}}
:=
\frac{\langle S,T\rangle_F}{\|T\|_F^2},
\qquad
\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}
:=
\frac{d_{\mathrm{eff}}}{\lambda^2 n^2}.
\]
The resulting hidden-space closure is
\[
BB^\top \approx \kappa_{\mathrm{eff}} T.
\]
Pushing through \(B^\top(\cdot)B\) gives the weighted square law
\[
H^2
\approx
\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}\widetilde G.
\]

If \(T\) is singular, let \(P_T\) be the projector onto \(\operatorname{range}(T)\).
The support-normalized pair diagnostic is
\[
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{pair}}
:=
\left\|
T^{\dagger/2}ST^{\dagger/2}
-
d_{\mathrm{eff}}P_T
\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
This quantity is intentionally worst-case. It can be large because
\(T^{\dagger/2}\) amplifies directions with small nonzero support. For this
reason we also monitor the pushed-forward pair error
\[
\left\|B^\top(S-d_{\mathrm{eff}}T)B\right\|_{\mathrm{op}},
\]
which is closer to the error entering the final weighted law.

\subsection{Deterministic weighted law}

Let
\[
E_{\mathrm{pair}}:=S-d_{\mathrm{eff}}T.
\]
Then
\[
H^2-\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}\widetilde G
=
B^\top
\left(
\frac{1}{\lambda^2 n^2}E_{\mathrm{pair}}
+
E_{\mathrm{stat}}
\right)B.
\]
Taking operator norms gives
\[
\|H^2-\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}\widetilde G\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le
\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2
\left(
\frac{\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{\lambda^2 n^2}
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{pair}}
+
\|E_{\mathrm{stat}}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\right).
\]
This is the deterministic theorem template tested by the experiments.

\subsection{Why the raw AGOP law becomes ill-conditioned}

The raw AGOP bridge compares
\[
M=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n q_iq_i^\top,
\qquad
\widetilde M=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n r_i^2q_iq_i^\top.
\]
Define the Frobenius best-fit scalar
\[
\beta_{\mathrm{fit}}
:=
\frac{\langle \widetilde M,M\rangle_F}{\|M\|_F^2}.
\]
Using
\[
\langle q_iq_i^\top,q_jq_j^\top\rangle_F
=
(q_i^\top q_j)^2,
\]
we get
\[
\beta_{\mathrm{fit}}
=
\frac{
\sum_{i,j} r_i^2(q_i^\top q_j)^2
}{
\sum_{i,j}(q_i^\top q_j)^2
}.
\]
Thus \(\beta_{\mathrm{fit}}\) is a nonnegative weighted average of the squared
residuals. In particular,
\[
\min_i r_i^2
\le
\beta_{\mathrm{fit}}
\le
\max_i r_i^2.
\]
As the model approaches interpolation, \(\beta_{\mathrm{fit}}\to 0\). Converting
the weighted law into a raw AGOP law then requires division by a small scalar,
which makes the raw relation ill-conditioned late in training.

\subsection{Empirical summary}

We test isotropic Gaussian, anisotropic Gaussian, and low-rank signal plus
isotropic noise families. Across all three families, the observed weighted
residual is small at best-stationarity checkpoints and the deterministic bound
is conservative on average. The raw support-normalized \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{pair}}\)
can be large, but the pushed-forward pair error is small. Across all logged
checkpoints, \(\beta_{\mathrm{fit}}\) closely tracks mean residual squared,
supporting the interpretation that the raw-to-weighted bridge scalar is
essentially training residual energy in these experiments.
